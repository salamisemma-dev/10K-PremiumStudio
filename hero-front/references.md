# Hero Front Reference Studies

Reference sites are studied for composition and restraint only. They are not templates and not clone targets.

## Duten

Source: `https://www.duten.com/en/`

Adopt:
- Product/material confidence: stainless steel, finish names, tactile precision.
- Hero language joins quality and minimal design.
- Background should give the object room and make surface detail visible.

Leave:
- Exact product framing, typography, finish names, and brand rhythm.

Pros:
- Excellent for premium product, architecture, wellness, craft, and industrial clients.

Cons and fixes:
- Con: Can become cold or showroom-flat.
  Fix: Add scene-specific light, human scale, or environmental trace when the client needs warmth.

## Breakfast

Source: `https://wearebreakfast.com/`

Adopt:
- Huge confident statement and energetic image rhythm.
- Culture-first visual texture that supports a bold creative promise.

Leave:
- Exact collage, music-world styling, repeated link strips, and brand voice.

Pros:
- Good for agencies, music, film, culture, launch, and youth-facing offers.

Cons and fixes:
- Con: Energy can become noise.
  Fix: Keep one focal subject and one typographic hierarchy; use image rhythm below the fold if the hero needs calm.

## Juice

Source: `https://www.juice.eu.com/`

Adopt:
- Clear agency promise and fast path to work.
- Portfolio-first visual proof after the hero.

Leave:
- Generic agency grid, exact claim, and any stock-like shopper imagery.

Pros:
- Useful when the proof is the work and the CTA is discovery.

Cons and fixes:
- Con: Agency grids can feel common.
  Fix: Make the hero image carry a specific client scene before showing work tiles.

## Rolls-Royce

Source: `https://www.rolls-roycemotorcars.com/en_GB/home.html`

Adopt:
- Full-bleed prestige, sparse copy, quiet onward journey, object as status carrier.

Leave:
- Automotive luxury codes when the client is not automotive.

Pros:
- Strong reference for elegance and controlled pacing.

Cons and fixes:
- Con: Prestige can become empty darkness.
  Fix: Tie the hero to proof, material, and a real client scene.
