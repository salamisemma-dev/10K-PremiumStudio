# Example Study: GoodVisuals + Rolls-Royce

This is a reference-study record, not a clone brief. Use it to inform route architecture and cinematic restraint under the scene-first law.

## Adopt

- GoodVisuals: proof-led clarity architecture with hero metaphor, proof band, outcome-over-output comparison, method, gates, impact ladder, offers, and diagnostic CTA.
- Rolls-Royce: full-bleed scene confidence, sparse copy, slow luxury pacing, and composed onward-journey cards.
- Multi-page premium structure when every route has one job and one anchor.

## Leave

- Exact colors, copy, animation metaphor, layout, and brand-specific components.
- Unsupported statistics or uncited proof.
- Decorative canvas without accessibility fallback.
- External font/CDN dependencies in delivered builds.
- Forms without real endpoint assumptions, names, labels, validation, and privacy context.

## Pros

- Gives future sites a stronger proof rhythm.
- Helps agents see that premium can be quiet but still commercially clear.
- Supports route-aware sites without losing the landing-page lead.

## Cons And Fixes

- Con: Reference study can become imitation.
  Fix: Record adopted and rejected lessons in `projects/<client>/brief.md`; the scene must explain every visual choice.
- Con: Multi-page sites can dilute focus.
  Fix: Require a visitor job and route message for every route.
- Con: Rich motion can hide weak content.
  Fix: One scene-expressive motion metaphor per route, with static fallback and reduced-motion support.
