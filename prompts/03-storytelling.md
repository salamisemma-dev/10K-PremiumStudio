# Prompt Blueprint: Storytelling & Narrative Structure

## Goal

Structure landing-page content as a story, not a feature dump. A premium page persuades
by taking the visitor on a logical, emotional path. This sits on top of two owners,
it does not replace them:

- Section roles (tension / proof / detail / action) are owned by
  `blueprints/03-design-laws.md` (Micro-Narrative). The four acts below map 1:1 to them.
- Word-level copy rules are owned by `prompts/02-copywriting.md` (3–5 word headlines,
  no em dash, proof over adjectives). Follow those when writing each act.

UX laws applied here come from `blueprints/06-ux-principles.md`.

## The four acts of a premium landing page

### Act 1 - The Hook (Hero) · role: tension
Stop the scroll with the one thing in a single clear line.
- Headline 3–5 words; optional subline ≤ 10 words; no explanation, a promise or an
  intriguing observation. Large type, one strong visual.
- UX law: **Peak–End** + **Nudge** - the first impression sets the whole experience.

### Act 2 - The Problem (Thesis) · role: proof (recognition)
Make the visitor feel understood.
- Name the specific problem you solve. Concrete, measurable pain. Max 3 sentences or a
  2–3 item list. No "in the old days" clichés.
- UX law: **Confirmation Bias** - people look for evidence that matches their feeling.

### Act 3 - The Solution (Features & proof) · role: detail
Show how you solve it, as benefits not specs.
- Max 3–4 core features; each one sentence + a number/fact where possible. Bullets only
  if truly needed.
- UX law: **Hick's Law** + **Miller's Law** - limit and group; don't overload.

### Act 4 - The Invitation (CTA) · role: action
One calm next step.
- Active but soft CTA text (e.g. "View the collection"); supporting line ≤ 10 words; no
  fake urgency, no "buy now" shouting.
- UX law: **Nudge** + **Peak–End** - make the wanted action easy; end deliberately.

## Master prompt

> Write the content for a premium landing page using the 4-act structure.
>
> Brand: [NAME] · One thing: [ONE SENTENCE] · Unique proof: [SPECIFIC FACT/NUMBER] ·
> Audience: [DESCRIPTION]
>
> 1. Act 1 (Hero): headline 3–5 words + optional subline.
> 2. Act 2 (Problem): max 3 sentences that make the problem recognizable.
> 3. Act 3 (Solution): max 4 features, each one sentence with a measurable benefit.
> 4. Act 4 (CTA): one calm invitation, max 5 words.
>
> Apply the UX laws (limit choices, match expectations, nudge gently). Obey
> `prompts/02-copywriting.md`. Derive nothing the client brief does not support.

## Story without words (visual support per act)

| Act | Visual support | UX law |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Hook | the strongest visual | Peak–End / Nudge |
| 2 Problem | calm, darker ground | Confirmation Bias |
| 3 Solution | lighter, open imagery | Hick / Miller |
| 4 Invitation | minimal closing visual | Nudge / Peak–End |

All visuals obey the 5-asset rule and scene lighting (`blueprints/01` + `brand/manifest.md`).
