# Blueprint: Design Laws

## Goal

Strengthen premium design decisions without adding trend rules, copied prompt styles, or unnecessary motion.

These laws are constraints. They exist to keep each website focused, cinematic, readable, and fast.

## 1. Micro-Narrative

Every section has one clear role in the story:

- tension
- proof
- detail
- action

A section may not explain, sell, prove, and close at the same time. If it tries to do more than one job, split it or remove the weaker idea.

## 2. Visual Anchor

Every page has one primary visual anchor. This can be a photo, video, render, 3D object, or typographic moment.

The anchor supports the `h1`. It may never block the core message, weaken contrast, or slow down the first meaningful impression.

## 3. Typographic Dominance

Premium pages use strong typographic scale.

The `h1` sets the hierarchy immediately. Labels stay small and quiet. Body copy is short, functional, and easy to scan.

Avoid decorative type treatments that reduce readability. Scale is allowed to be bold; clarity is not optional.

## 4. Restraint Before Spectacle

Motion is allowed only when it improves understanding, focus, or premium feel.

Use a small number of memorable motion moments per page. Constant animation, decorative scroll effects, and motion without purpose make the experience cheaper.

## 5. Performance-Preserved Motion

Animations should use `transform` and `opacity` where possible.

Complex motion needs a mobile fallback. Reduced-motion users receive a calm static experience. A motion idea that cannot preserve performance and accessibility does not ship.
